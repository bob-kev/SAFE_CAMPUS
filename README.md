# 🚨 SafeCampus — Campus Emergency Reporting Mobile App

*HCI Group Project — Group 9*

Design a mobile application that enables users to report
emergencies and safety incidents quickly and clearly.

---

## Project Overview

SafeCampus is a mobile app prototype for university students and
staff to report emergencies and safety incidents in a fast, clear,
and reliable way. Because people face stress during emergencies,
the app prioritizes minimal interaction, large touch targets, clear
feedback, and error prevention.

- User-centered design driven by real user research
- 12+ interactive Balsamiq wireframe screens
- Usability tested with 5 real users
- Iterative improvements based on feedback

*Platform:* Smartphones (portrait focus)
*Tools:* Balsamiq, GitHub

---

## Problem Statement

On campus, people who witness or face an emergency often don't know
who to contact, how to report it, or what to say. Existing options
(phone numbers, office visits, forms) are slow, confusing, and give
no confirmation — leading to delayed help and under-reporting.

## Project Objectives

1. Report an incident in 3 taps or fewer
2. Guide users through a clear, structured report
3. Auto-share location to reduce effort and error
4. Prevent false/accidental reports via confirmation
5. Give instant confirmation and status feedback
6. Ensure accessibility, privacy, and trust

## Target Users

University students (18–30) and staff who may witness or experience
campus incidents. Research with 5 users explored current reporting
behavior, fears, and what would encourage reporting.

## Key Features

- Emergency type selection (Fire, Medical, Threat, Accident, etc.)
- One-tap "Call Emergency/Security"
- Automatic location sharing
- Guided report form (description, people involved, status)
- QuickTap one-tap critical report
- Confirmation & trackable status with report ID
- Safety resources section

## HCI Principles Applied

Learnability, Efficiency, Error Prevention, Feedback, Accessibility,
Privacy/Trust, and Stress-aware design (calm colors, big buttons,
low cognitive load).
